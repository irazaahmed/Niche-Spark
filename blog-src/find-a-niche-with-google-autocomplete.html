Picking a niche is the decision that shapes everything after it: the content you write, the keywords you can win, and how the site eventually earns. Google Autocomplete won't tell you a niche is profitable on its own, but it is one of the fastest free ways to compare ideas using what people actually search for.

This guide gives you a repeatable process: brainstorm candidates, run quick autocomplete research on each, score them on the same checklist, then validate the best one with checks autocomplete can't do.

What makes a niche worth building

A workable niche usually has four things:

  • Demand: people search the topic in many different ways, not just one head term.
  • Buyer intent: some of those searches involve spending money: prices, "best", reviews, services.
  • A clear audience: you know who is searching and what they want.
  • Room to compete: the results aren't completely owned by huge brands.

Autocomplete is strong on the first three. The fourth needs a look at the actual search results, covered later.

Step 1: Brainstorm 5 to 10 candidate seeds

Write down specific topics you could cover well. Specific beats broad: mobile car detailing is a niche; cars is an industry. Good sources of ideas include your own skills and interests, local services, hobbies with equipment, and problems people pay to solve.

Step 2: Run a quick A to Z pass on each

For each candidate, run the A to Z method: seed plus each letter, 26 queries. You're not building a full keyword list yet. You're looking at the shape of each niche. Use the same Google domain and language for every candidate so the comparison is fair.

Step 3: Probe buyer intent with modifiers

Add a small, fixed set of prefixes and suffixes to every candidate, for example:

  • Prefixes: best, cheap
  • Suffixes: cost, near me, reviews, for beginners

Using the same modifiers everywhere keeps the comparison honest. A niche where cost and best return rich, specific predictions has buyers. A niche where they return little or unrelated phrases may be harder to monetize.

Step 4: Score each candidate

Read each candidate's results and score them from 0 to 2 on five questions. Here's the sheet we use:

A candidate scoring 8 to 10 deserves a closer look. Below 5, move on unless you have a special advantage in that topic.

Score what you see, not what you hope. The point of using the same queries and the same sheet for every idea is to stop your favorite idea from winning by default.

Step 5: Look for hidden audiences

Autocomplete often shows that one seed serves more than one audience. In our mobile car detailing case study, most searches came from customers (prices, "near me", city names), but a clear second group came from people starting a detailing business: business for sale, start up cost, equipment, insurance, jobs.

That matters for niche selection. Two audiences can mean two sites, or one site with a clear focus. Decide which one you're serving before you write anything, because the content, keywords and monetization are completely different.

Step 6: Validate what autocomplete can't show

Autocomplete gives you real phrasing and themes. Before committing, add three checks it can't do:

  1. Check the search results. Search a handful of your best long-tail keywords. If the first page is full of forums, small sites and thin pages, there's room. If it's all major brands with deep content, it will be slow going.
  2. Check volume. Google says autocomplete doesn't simply show the most common queries, so use a keyword metrics tool to size your best groups.
  3. Check monetization. Look at how existing sites earn: services, affiliate products, ads, lead generation. If you can't see a path, that's a warning.

Step 7: Turn the winner into a plan

Once a niche passes, go deeper: run more prefixes and suffixes on its strongest themes, group the keywords by topic and intent, and give each group one page. The complete autocomplete guide covers grouping and avoiding keyword cannibalization.

A practical start for a new site: publish a few strong pages before you promote anything, then keep adding pages on a steady schedule, interlinking them as you go.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Judging from one query. The seed's first predictions hide most of the niche. Always run the full pass.
  • Comparing across different markets. Predictions differ by country and language; compare candidates on the same Google.
  • Counting noise as demand. Brand names and far-away cities inflate the list without adding opportunity.
  • Skipping the results check. Demand without room to compete is a trap.

Frequently asked questions

Can autocomplete prove a niche is profitable?

No. It shows real demand, phrasing and intent. Profitability also depends on competition, volume and how the niche earns, which you check separately.

How many niche ideas should I compare?

Five to ten is a good range. Fewer, and you may miss a better option; more, and the comparison takes long enough that you stop being consistent.

Should I pick a niche I know?

Knowledge helps you write better, more trustworthy content faster. Use the scoring sheet to check that a topic you know also has the demand and intent to support a site.

How long does this process take?

The autocomplete part is quick, especially with a tool that runs the queries for you. The validation checks take longer, and that's where the real decision gets made.
